How booking options work

After you choose General ticketing or Application based, you pick one booking path. Use the quick pick below, then open an option for the detail.

Which sounds like you?

  • One-off or ticketed day? → Ticket for this event
  • Weekly group, try then join? → Free visits, then membership
  • Visitors buy tickets; members at a member rate? → Ticket and membership
1 Ticket for this event Best for one-off or simple paid or free booking 2 Free visits, then membership Best for weekly groups with try-before-you-join Popular for weekly groups 3 Ticket and membership Best when visitors buy tickets and members book at a member rate

Option 1

Ticket for this event

Choose this if you simply want people to book this meeting — free or paid — with a straightforward ticket name and price.

What attendees see

Your ticket types on the event page. They choose a tier and book that ticket for that date.

What you set up

  • Ticket name, free or paid price, optional description
  • Optional limit on how many are available (leave blank for unlimited)

What you get

A clear guest list for that event. No monthly membership and no free-visit programme on this path.

Example — exhibition or conference day

  • Early bird £49 Lower rate until a sale end date you set
  • General admission £79 Standard day ticket once early bird ends
  • VIP / lunch included £129 Optional extras as their own ticket type

You choose the names and prices — these figures are only to show how several ticket types work.

Option 2

Free visits, then membership

Choose this if you run a regular group and want visitors to try a few meetings, then join your membership — so you keep their visit history and reports in one place.

  1. 1
    Free trial visits

    Visitors attend a few times without joining (you choose how many, up to 3).

  2. 2
    Member ticket for this meeting

    People on your member list book at your member price (often £0).

  3. 3
    Monthly or annual join fee

    After visits are used, visitors pay to join your group. That fee is yours; we only add our booking fee on the join payment.

What attendees see

A chance to try your meetings first. When visits are used, a clear next step to join your group on the Hub — not a surprise commitment, and not “signing up to The Networker Hub” as a product.

What you set up

  • How many free trial visits (up to 3)
  • Member ticket price for this meeting (often £0)
  • Monthly or annual membership fee after visits are used

What you get

  • Visit history for people who tried your meetings
  • A member list under Memberships
  • Reports on who came, who joined, and who is ready to pay

Example — weekly networking breakfast

  • Free trial visits 2 free Visitors try two meetings before joining
  • Member ticket £0 Members book each meeting at no extra charge
  • Monthly membership £35 a month Join fee after free visits — covers ongoing meetings

Prices are examples — you set your own. No public visitor ticket on this path; guests use free visits, then join.

Benefits of running membership through the Hub

  • You keep 100% of the membership price you set — members pay a separate booking fee (4.5% + 20p) on top; that is not taken from your fee.
  • Join, renew and cancel in one place — members manage their card and renewal from My Hub; you manage the list under Memberships.
  • Visit history and reports — see who tried the group, who joined, who has booked, and who is due to renew.
  • Your brand, your group — people join your membership on your organiser page, not a Hub subscription.
  • Invite to pay — ask people on your list to pay the join fee when you are ready, without chasing bank transfers by hand.

Option 3

Ticket and membership

Choose this if visitors should buy a ticket for this event (for example meeting + lunch), while members attend on their membership — often at £0 because the fee already covers it.

What attendees see

Visitors buy a public ticket for this date. Members book the member rate (often £0) because membership already includes the meeting — and lunch, if you include it.

What you set up

  • Public ticket types for visitors (paid or free)
  • Optional free trial visits, then your monthly or annual membership fee
  • Member ticket price for people on your list (often £0)

What you get

Ticket sales from visitors and a membership path for regulars — with visit history and reports in one place.

Example — breakfast networking with lunch add-on

  • Free trial visits 2 free Visitors try two meetings before joining
  • Visitor — meeting + lunch £18 Public ticket so visitors can add lunch on the day
  • Member ticket £0 Members get lunch included in their membership
  • Monthly membership £35 a month Join fee after free visits — covers meetings and lunch

Visitors pay for the ticket (and lunch). Members already covered by membership book at £0. Prices are examples — you set your own.

Membership fee handling, visit history, and “your brand, your group” work the same as Option 2 — see Benefits of running membership through the Hub.

Still not sure?

Most weekly networking groups use Free visits, then membership.
